# Loot-Council (`lootcouncil` area)

Endnutzer-Sicht: siehe [guide-web-admin.md#loot-council](guide-web-admin.md#loot-council).

The loot council — casters, healers, tanks, melee and hunters: per raider, what they were given lately, how far their gear still is from BiS, and what a given drop would be worth to them — the page a council argues over when a boss dies. `src/web/loot/lootCouncil.js` derives all of it **on read** from data the bot already keeps; nothing is stored.

- **The raid-category filter narrows the *roster*, not just the loot** (a category without a roster; with one, see the next point). Filtering only the items left every other raid's casters standing there with "0 Items" and a maximum drought — on top of the very ranking the page exists for. `categoryMembers()` decides who belongs, from three sources unioned because none is complete on its own:
  1. **the logs of that category's raids** (`categoryFromReports()`: Report → Log `reportRefId` → `eventId` → raid event → `categoryId`) — the strongest, because nobody maintains it: whoever stands in a Monday log raids on Mondays;
  2. **the loot awarded there** — covers raids that were never evaluated, but is blind to anyone who never won something;
  3. **the raider→character assignment** (`raiderCharactersStore`, Einstellungen → Kategorien) — the only source that knows a raider who has neither won nor been logged there.

  ⚠️ **When all three find nobody, the filter still applies and the roster is empty.** Falling back to "show everyone" was the original bug: picking a category then changed nothing, which is precisely what it is supposed to do. `filter.categorySources` reports what each source contributed, so the page can say *what to fix* — an empty list with an explanation is useful, a full list is not.
- **Who is on the council in a category with a roster (#667): the roster is the candidate list**, on the page and in game. `web/loot/councilRosterSource.js` `rosterCandidates(categoryId, { showBench })` reads `rosterForCategory()` and `councilRoster()` takes its characters instead of the loot ∪ gear pool; `categoryMembers()` then only finds the stand-ins. The rules (epic #671, 09.10.2026):
  - **Stamm and Probe are candidates**, equally — in practice the two are hardly told apart. **Ersatz** only with the page's switch "Ersatz zeigen" (`?bench=1` → `opts.showBench`; a browser preference in `VIEW_KEY`, never in the stored category view, so the addon never gets the bench). **Pause** never — it counts like "Nicht eingeplant" (`skipped.paused`; hidden bench as `skipped.bench`).
  - **Every assigned character with `allowMultipleChars`**; without it a member has one anyway. **The keys come from `member.chars`** (profile keys of the roster's version, "forever~devi res"), but every data source — loot, logs, gear, character store, the set-aside list — keys by the bare lower-case name, so the council key is `nameKeyOf()` of it. A roster holds one version, so its characters never collide on that. A character two members name gets the stronger status. A roster member's game version is the roster's (no derivation).
  - **The fixed bug:** a member with neither loot nor gear never appeared — the pool was built from loot and gear only. Now they are rows with 0 items, `daysSinceLoot: null` and therefore full drought (`needScore` counts "never" as 30 days). Their class and spec may come from **the member's profile character** as the last source (`classAndSpec()`: characterInfo → character store → gear → profile; of several profile specs the one of the role asked for).
  - **Status as information:** a roster row carries `status` (`core`/`trial`/`bench`); the page shows Probe/Ersatz as a badge (`StatusBadge` in `RosterList.tsx`, also in the dialog head). Without a roster the rows carry no `status` at all, and `councilRoster()`'s answer is byte-for-byte the old one (`lootCouncil.golden.test.js`). The addon payload stays version 2 — the status ships with #670.
  - **Without a council spec:** a candidate `specFor()` cannot place (a warrior, a priest without a spec) is not dropped silently but listed in `rosterSource.noSpec`; the page counts them in a badge with the names in the tooltip. Left out by the role filter is not "unknown".
  - **Stand-ins:** characters the category's logs or loot know (`categoryMembers()`) who are in the roster under no status and not set aside come back as `outsiders` — full rows without a need score, folded under "Nicht im Roster (n)" (`RosterTab.tsx` `OutsiderFold`). They are not in `rows`, so they are neither ranked, nor part of the loot-share average, nor drop candidates, nor in the addon (`councilSync.js` reads `rows`).
  - `rosterSource` (`{ id, name, versionId, counts, showBench, noSpec }`, on the page `filter.roster`) names the roster for the filter line's badge; `categoryNote()` steps back for it.
- **Raiders can be set aside** (`councilStore.js`, `POST /api/lootcouncil/exclude`). Someone who left the guild wins the "hat am längsten nichts bekommen" ranking simply by not raiding — their drought grows forever. Excluding is reversible, remembers who and when, and never touches the loot history. Deliberately explicit rather than an automatic "inactive for 60 days" rule: the difference between *gone* and *was ill* is one only a person knows.
- **Who is on it** — class and spec come from three sources in order: `characterInfo` (annotates only raiders with loot), then `characterStore` (knows everyone from the log evaluations), then the report's roster (class only). All three are needed: a raider who has *never won an item* is exactly the case the council is looking for, and only the last two know them. A class whose spec nothing settles (a priest could be shadow or holy, a warrior fury or protection) is **dropped rather than guessed** — a wrong spec means a wrong BiS list. `config/councilSpecs.js` holds the spec table, the stat weights and the hit caps (see „Rollen und Specs“ below; its old name `config/casterSpecs.js` is gone).
- **What their gear is** — `src/services/loot/charGear.js` reads the armory out of the stored CLA reports (`data/reports/*.json`), which already carry item id, gems and the permanent-enchant id per slot from Warcraft Logs. That is *last seen*, not current: every payload carries `seenAt`, and a raider in no report has no gear (the page says so instead of simulating a naked character).
- **…and that it is the right *kind* of gear.** Shamans, druids and priests heal a night regularly, so their newest log may show a healing set — which would give a DPS caster no DPS worth the name and let every drop "replace" a healing piece it has nothing to do with. `src/services/loot/gearProfile.js` tells the two apart from the stats: on a damage item WoWSims models healing power and spell power as *equal*, while a healing item carries far more healing (a T6 shadow set sits at 866/866, a healing set at 7605/2537), and only a damage set chases the hit cap. Both signals are weighed together, and `charGear`'s `roleFor` then walks back to the newest raid where the raider actually played that role, reporting `skippedReports`. A set that is genuinely ambiguous is *accepted* rather than rejected — leaving a raider with no gear is worse than slightly odd gear — and one that never fits anywhere is used with `roleMismatch`, so the page can say the numbers rest on healing gear instead of silently pretending otherwise.
- **Off-spec rolls, shards and bank items do not count as loot** (`countsAsLoot` in `utils/loot/lootReasons.js`). They did nothing for the raider's set, and counting them would rank somebody who politely took three shards above a raider who got one real upgrade — the fairness half of the ranking would be exactly backwards. They are tallied separately as `otherCount` and named in the hover rather than hidden, so the number does not look wrong to whoever handed them out. `other` (an unrecognised response) *does* count: it is far more often a guild's own wording for a mainspec roll than it is a shard.
- **What BiS is — two sources, and `src/config/bisSets.js` is the only place that reads them.** `src/config/generated/wowsims/` is generated by `node scripts/fetch-wowsims-data.js` from wowsims/tbc-new (MIT): the item DB, the BiS sets per spec and tier, and the rotations. ⚠️ **WoWSims-TBC ships no healer BiS at all** — every healing gear set there is an empty placeholder and there is no priest healing sim — so the five healing specs come from **Wowhead's written BiS guides** instead (`src/config/generated/wowhead/bisSets.json`, `node scripts/fetch-wowhead-bis.js`). Neither is hand-edited. WoWSims wins wherever it has something, so a spec never silently swaps from a simulated list to a written one, and every answer carries `source` so the page can label a Wowhead column as what it is. **The difference is real and must stay visible: a Wowhead guide names items and nothing else** — no gems, no enchants — which is enough for the BiS gap and the stat weights, and not enough to simulate against.
- **Refreshing the lists is `npm run bis:refresh`, and it runs the Wowhead scraper twice on purpose.** The scraper needs the item table to know which slot a piece goes in; the item table needs the scraper's ids to carry a healing relic at all, because an idol, totem or libram has *no stats* — its whole value is an effect — and `isRaidItem` would drop it. So pass one writes down what it could not place (`pending`), `fetch-wowsims-data.js` takes those ids along (above the quality cut only: a written guide may name an item the table filters for want of stats, never a green), and pass two resolves them. Two further things the scraper does that are easy to get wrong, both covered by `test/scripts/fetchWowheadBis.test.js`: everything behind a `[toggler]` is dropped (the guide itself calls those the fallbacks), and **which slot a piece takes comes from the item, not the section heading** — the item table lists both hands for every weapon, so without narrowing that the Weapons section's second-best entry took the off hand and the actual off-hand piece had nowhere to go.
- **BiS is per spec *and* tier, never per item.** WoWSims has five caster lines (Shadow, Arcane, Destruction, Balance, Elemental); Fire/Frost borrow Arcane's and the two other warlock specs borrow Destruction's, marked as borrowed wherever shown. Most drops are contested — 29 of the 50 items on a T6 caster list are wanted by more than one spec — so a bare "BiS" badge answers nothing. `bisSpecsForItem()` (councilSpecs.js) says *whose*, folding a borrowing spec into the chip of the list it borrows so a contested item shows five claims rather than nine rows; `bisSpecsView()` adds the spec icon and class colour, and it rides on every item the page draws: BiS gaps, the drop check, and each worn piece.
- **Which BiS list to measure against** defaults to the tier the guild's *newest loot* comes from (`currentTier()`), not to the newest list WoWSims has — holding a T6 guild against Sunwell gear makes everyone look equally far from BiS and says nothing. The payload reports which tier was used and whether it was derived.
- **The need score** (`needScore()`) has four parts — drought, loot share, BiS gap and belonging — weighted by the council (default 45 / 30 / 10 / 15, `NEED_WEIGHTS`; see "Gewichtung und Zugehörigkeit" below), and its parts go out with it so the page can show the reasoning — the bar is stacked in exactly those weights (the answer's `weights.needShares`, `NeedWeightsProvider` in `lootcouncil/needWeights.ts`), so it shows not just how overdue somebody is but which part drives it. It rides along on every *candidate* too, not just on the roster row: "who would gain most" and "who has waited longest" are two different questions, shown as two bars side by side. Folding them into one number would look like an answer and hide the judgement the council is there to make.
- **The gear itself** goes out with each raider (`gear.items`, character-sheet order) and behind each candidate's replaced slot (`replaces`), through `wornItemView()`: the log's own name and icon — it saw what they actually wear — plus stats, raid and BiS membership from the item table. The page draws it as a row of icons under the raider, since sixteen item names are unreadable and sixteen icons are a character sheet.
- **A candidate the drop is not BiS for counts half.** `candidateSplit()` puts `bisWeight` (1, or `NON_BIS_WEIGHT` = 0.5) and `itemNeedScore` (= need × weight) on every candidate; the server's ordering and the page's verdict, gain sort and need sort all multiply by it, while the displayed DPS delta and the need bar stay the raw numbers. So a raider for whom the item is BiS is preferred unless the other's measured gain is more than twice as large — the item still *helps* the other, it is just not theirs first.
- **Hit is capped, not linear**: `upgradeValue()` stops counting hit past the raider's cap on *both* sides of a swap, so a capped raider is neither handed the hit trinket nor punished for losing hit they did not need. Which hit counts is `hitStatFor()`: spell hit for a caster, physical hit (`meleeHit`, the one rating the item table carries for melee and ranged) for melee, hunters and tanks, none for a healer. The roster row carries it as `gear.hit` / `gear.hitStat` / `gear.hitCap`.
- **A raider who cannot equip the item is never a candidate.** `src/config/wearable.js` holds the game's rules — the item's class list (tier/set pieces), armour type per class (cloth classes get no leather/mail, the back slot is exempt), weapon type and hand per class (no two-hander for a rogue, no sword for a priest, a shaman's two-handed axe only as Enhancement), and the ranged/relic type (wands for cloth, one relic type per class). It reads the `classes`/`armorType`/`weaponType`/`rangedType` fields of the generated item table, so they must survive a re-run of `fetch-wowsims-data.js`. `candidateSplit()` in `lootCouncil.js` returns `{ candidates, unwearable }` — the drop check lists the unwearable raiders with the reason instead of silently shortening the list — and `simStore.js` skips such pairs too, because a simulated number for a piece the raider cannot put on is a wrong answer however well measured. An item the table does not know passes: "cannot tell" is not "cannot wear".
- **No estimates.** The page shows a gain only once it is simulated; a candidate without a result says "nicht simuliert", an item nobody in the list can be simulated for (healers) is suggested by need and labelled so, and the stat-weight `value` stays in the payload only as the server's ordering. A picked drop is simulated automatically against its candidates (seconds); the whole BiS list stays a button (minutes).
- **PvP gear is refused as a source.** Between two raid nights the armory shows the arena set, and resilience is worth nothing to a boss. `gearProfile.js`'s `isPvpSet()` (half or more of the known pieces carry resilience) makes `charGear.js` reject an armory set with `armoryRejected: "pvp"` (a healing set for a caster: `"role"`) and keep the last raid's gear, skip PvP sets in the report walk like wrong-role sets, and fall back to one only when nothing else exists (`pvpGear: true`). `armoryItemInSlot()` refuses a single PvP piece for the same reason. The page's gear stamp and the armory toast say which.
- **A third gear source: one Warcraft-Logs report, loaded by hand** (`src/stores/logGearStore.js`, `POST /api/lootcouncil/loggear`). The evaluations only know raids somebody ran the CLA on, and the armory only knows *now* (arena set, Blizzard enchant ids). The "Log" segment of the gear-source switch in the raider's details opens a panel listing the bot's newest logs (`recentLogs` in the council payload) plus a link field; the server reads that report's casts table once, builds the raider's armory with the CLA's own `buildArmory` (gems, enchant status, boss-specific pieces resolved against the same night) and keeps it on disk under the character. `charGear.js` then uses it with `source: "wcl"` — it replaces the evaluation's set, gives gear to a raider no evaluation knows, and **yields to an evaluation newer than the request** (`report.generatedAt > snapshot.fetchedAt`), which is the natural end of "the set from Thursday". The same role/PvP rules as for the armory apply (`logRejected: "pvp" | "role"` keeps the evaluation's set and the stamp says so); the armory still wins over a loaded log, so loading one drops that raider's armory cache entry (`clearArmoryFor`), and "Auswertung" (`clear: true`) drops both. Without a report id the newest `MAX_LOGS` logs are tried in turn, and the error names how many.
- **Layout: list, details dialog, drop page** (design issue #223, canvas https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9f0bbaaa-5c6b-422a-a5ea-a60f2bddfbf9). `lootcouncil/LootCouncilPage.tsx` is the page head, the five tabs first (Gewichtung since #668) and then ONE filter line under them (`lootcouncil/FilterBar.tsx`: the role segment Caster | Heiler | Tank | Nahkampf | Fernkampf | Alle and a single "Filter · n aktiv" button; its box holds Content, Raid-Kategorie and BiS-Liste, which apply live and are what the count adds up, plus two read-only facts, Gear-Quelle and Simulation — the former "Gear: Auswertung" / "Sim aus" chips; "Zurücksetzen" clears the three filters), no card header repeating the tab (the Raider tab keeps just its ordering rule and "DPS berechnen" in `.lc-rosterbar`); numbers come with words ("Bedarf 82", "BiS 9 von 16", "vor 3 Tagen" under "Letztes Item"); the Raider tab is one compact line per raider (`lootcouncil/RosterList.tsx`). Everything about one raider — gear with the source segment (Auswertung · Log · Armory), badges instead of the gear stamp, the character sheet, BiS gaps, loot, log panel, role switch, Sim-Export (a second modal), Nicht einplanen, Armory — lives in `lootcouncil/RaiderDialog.tsx`, opened from `?raider=<name>` so a link leads straight there. "Drop prüfen" is its own route, `/lootcouncil/drop/:itemId?` (`lootcouncil/DropCheckPage.tsx`), reading the same persisted filters (`VIEW_KEY`); a stored `tab: "drop"` falls back to the Raider tab. With a raid category picked, role, Content and BiS list are the category's own, stored on the server for the whole orga and used by the in-game addon too (`lootcouncil/categoryViews.ts`, badge „Gilt auch fürs Spiel“; details in docs/loot-import.md, „Council-Daten für das Addon, Version 2“). Shared rules and the sim runner are in `lootcouncil/council.ts`, the tabs in `lootcouncil/RosterTab.tsx`, `GapsTab.tsx` (with `GapCard.tsx`), `BisListsTab.tsx` and `CompareTab.tsx`, the shared components (both routes draw them) in `lootcouncil/ItemBits.tsx`, `NeedBar.tsx`, `GearBadges.tsx` and `CandidateTable.tsx`, the CSS in `styles/loot-council.css`. Tooltips with more than a sentence (the need bar's four parts, the last items) are the shared `RichTip` (`components/ui/RichTip.tsx`, also the roster's attendance tooltip) with the class `lc-rtip`, in the shared `.tip` box, portalled into an open dialog. A worn item's icon is a Wowhead link carrying the raider's gems and enchant (`gemIds`/`enchantId` on `WornItem`, `wornWowheadUrl()`), so the widget tooltip (`lib/wow/wowheadTooltips.ts`, `refreshWowheadLinks()` after every render) shows the piece as worn; the corner marks stay and explain themselves in the tooltip box, so there is no legend.
- **Per game version** (#542): the council looks at its category's version (`mainVersionFor({ categoryId })`, else the main version) — the armory links (`versionLinks`), the realm of "Armory" (`primeArmoryGear(names, { versionId })`, a version without realm asks nobody and says so) and the Wowhead links: the payload carries `versionId`/`wowheadPath`, both pages wrap in `WowheadPathProvider`, `ItemLink`/`ItemHead`/`WornIcon` read `useWowheadPath()` and show the item without a link when the version has no path.
- **Every wait is a job toast** (`components/shell/Jobs.tsx`, `useJobs().run`): reloads and the drop fetch run `quiet` (the toast disappears on success), the armory and the simulation report their outcome, and the simulation feeds its real progress (`update({ progress })`) into the toast bar. The page keeps no progress bar or error line of its own — it is three screens long, and feedback under the filter section is feedback nobody sees.
- **A drop is judged against every slot it could go in**, not one. `targetSlotFor()` returns `options` (all candidate slots with what sits there), `displaces` (what would actually come off) and `clears`. Two cases it exists for: a doubled slot shows *both* rings/trinkets with the one that goes marked — which one is kept is half the decision; and a **two-handed weapon takes both hands**, so it displaces the off-hand piece as well. ⚠️ `clears` must reach the simulation (`simStore.js` passes it into the swap): without it WoWSims keeps the off hand equipped next to the staff and reports DPS off gear the raider cannot wear. The stat-weight value subtracts the off-hand piece for the same reason.

- **Reading a raider's gear costs a walk over every stored evaluation, so it happens once per request.** `gearByCharacter()` (charGear.js) opens every report under `data/reports/`; `bisGaps()` used to ask for it *per gap item* through `candidatesForItem()`, which on a real guild's data — a hundred gaps against forty 2-3 MB reports — turned the page into minutes of JSON parsing. The roster's gear is now built once (`rosterGear()`) and handed into `candidateSplit(itemId, roster, gearMap)`; the default argument only covers the single-item case (the drop check). **Anything that asks for more than one item must pass the map in.** Two things in `reportStore.js` keep the rest cheap: `listReports()` remembers each file's metadata (keyed by mtime + size, so a rebuilt report re-reads itself and nothing has to be invalidated by hand), and `getReportRoster(id)` hands out only the slice the gear walk reads — roster, players and three header fields, a twentieth of the file, the rest being timeline. `charGear`, `charGearIssues` and `characterInfo` all use it; anything that needs the timeline still takes `getReport()`.

## Gewichtung und Zugehörigkeit (#668)

Not every item is worth the same, and how long somebody has been part of the raid counts. Both are the council's settings, not constants.

- **Loot points.** Every counted award (`countsAsLoot`) carries a weight instead of 1: `services/loot/itemWeights.js` `itemWeight(itemId, settings, { bisIds, itemName })` → `{ weight, cls }`. The first rule that applies wins: (1) a per-item exception (`override`); (2) `frequent` — the item is listed under two or more loot buckets of `config/generated/tbcRaidLoot.json`, or under a non-boss bucket (`Trash`, Zul'Aman's `Timed Chest`; `NON_BOSSES` in `tbcContent.js`). First on purpose: an item that drops all the time is cheap whatever slot it goes in. In the current table every id sits in one bucket only, so in practice this is trash and the timed chest; (3) `trinket` — a trinket slot in the item table (WCL slots **12/13**, in-game 13/14); (4) `bisWeapon` — the item has a `weapon` damage block (one-/two-hand, off-hand weapon, wand, bow, gun; shields, held-in-off-hand items and relics have none) and is on the *recipient's* BiS list; (5) `weapon` — any other weapon; (6) `set` — `setName` in the WoWSims table, or a tier token by name (`tokenTier()`: "… of the Fallen/Vanquished/Forgotten Champion/Defender/Hero/Conqueror/Protector/Vanquisher", English and German; the name comes from the item table, `tbcLootNames.json` or the loot row); (7) `normal` — everything else, including unknown ids. A row carries `lootPoints` beside `lootCount`, every listed item `weight`/`weightClass`, and the share part compares points: `share = clamp((avgPoints − points) / max(1, avgPoints))` over the rows (stand-ins outside a roster are not rows and never count).
- **Drought with a partial reset** (`droughtDays(awards, daysSinceLoot)`). A counter D in days (cap 30) walks the awards oldest first: it starts full (never got anything = full drought), grows by the days between two awards (capped), and at an award of weight w becomes `D × max(0, 1 − w)`. After the newest award it grows by the whole days since. So weight ≥ 1 resets completely (exactly the old `min(30, days since last item)`), 0.5 halves the wait, 0 leaves it alone; weights above 1 cannot reset more than fully — they count extra in the share instead. Example: nothing ever, then a 0.5 item 4 days ago → 30 × 0.5 + 4 = 19 days. The row's `droughtDays` is that number, `daysSinceLoot` stays the calendar.
- **Belonging ("dabei seit")** — `services/loot/councilTenure.js` `tenureContext({ categoryId, allLoot, now })` returns `joinedAtFor(key)` → `{ joinedAt, source }`: the **earliest** of the roster (`member.since` and the oldest history line about that user; one date for all the person's characters — `since` is the last *status* change and migrated rosters share one date, hence "earliest"), the first raid of the category (a stored event whose log evaluation lists the character — newest 80 logs, roster slice only — or a `signed`/`late` signup of the member's account) and the first award in the category (any reason). Without a category every roster, raid and award counts; nothing known → `joinedAt: 0` and the part is 0. `tenure = min(1, tenureDays / saturation)`, saturation default 90 days. Rows carry `joinedAt`, `joinedFrom` (`roster`/`raid`/`loot`/""), `tenureDays`.
- **Normalisation.** The council edits whole numbers 0..100 per part; `effectiveNeedWeights()` divides by their sum, so the score stays 0..1 whatever is set (all zero falls back to the defaults). `score = Σ share_i × part_i`.
- **Settings** — `stores/councilWeightsStore.js` (`settings/council-weights.json`): `{ global, categories: { [id]: … } }`, each `{ classes: { trinket, bisWeapon, weapon, set, normal, frequent }, items: { [itemId]: { weight, name } }, need: { drought, share, need, tenure }, tenureDays, at, by }`, everything clamped (weights 0..5 on a 0.1 grid, ≤ 200 exceptions, tenure 7..365 days). A category's own settings replace the server's *as a whole* (`weightsFor(categoryId)`, `scope`). `councilRoster()` resolves them per request (`opts.weights` for tests) and answers `weights` (classes, items, need, `needShares`, `tenureDays`, `droughtDays`) next to `avgLootPoints`. Routes: `GET /api/lootcouncil/weights?category=` (read; plus `itemInfo` per exception: name, icon, the class it would have without it) and `POST /api/lootcouncil/weights` `{ category?, weights }` / `{ category?, reset: true }` (write).
- **Page** — tab "Gewichtung" (`lootcouncil/WeightsTab.tsx`): four sections (Item-Gewichte, Ausnahmen with the council item search, Bedarf, Zugehörigkeit), −/+ steppers, one primary "Speichern", "Auf Vorgaben zurücksetzen" only fills the form; with a category a switch gives it its own weighting; readers see it without controls. The list shows "n Items · x Punkte" (`LootCount` with `points`), the dialog points, a weight chip per item and "Dabei seit".
- **Addon (v1/v2 unchanged in shape).** `need` and the parts now include the weighting and tenure, `weights` carries the real drought/share/need shares in % (with the defaults 45/30/10 — the missing 15 is tenure, already inside `need`). Format v3 (#670) will add what is prepared here: `itemWeight()`/`itemClass()` per item, `lootPoints`, `needParts.tenure`, `joinedAt`/`tenureDays` and `weights` (classes, exceptions, shares) per category.

**The simulation is optional and always labelled.** `src/utils/wowsims/engine.js` runs the real `wowsimcli` binary as a local subprocess (`WOWSIMCLI_PATH`, fetched with `node scripts/fetch-wowsimcli.js`); without it every call answers `{ available: false }` and the page falls back to its stat weights, which is the normal state in CI and in a fresh worktree. ⚠️ The binary version, `WOWSIMS_VERSION` in `engine.js` and `SIM_VERSION` in `fetch-wowsims-data.js` are **one pin** — the protojson schema, the vendored rotations and the embedded item DB hang together (`test/utils/wowsims/engine.test.js` holds them in sync). `src/utils/wowsims/presets.js` is a 1:1 copy of WoWSims' own preset sets; a spec's set deliberately omits the buff it supplies itself (no Misery for the shadow priest), or it would count twice and inflate that spec against the others.

Runs are cached in `data/sim/` keyed by the loadout itself (`simStore.js`), so a changed gem re-simulates automatically and nothing else does; the seed is pinned (`randomSeed: "1"`) so an item that changes nothing reports a delta of exactly zero instead of sim noise. A council run is a background job the client polls — a whole roster against a full gap list is minutes of CPU.

**A swapped-in drop is simulated as it would be worn, never bare.** `loadout.js`'s `bisFittingFor()` gives it the gems and enchant of the spec's WoWSims BiS list: the list's own fitting when the item is on it, otherwise the enchant the list puts on that slot and, per socket colour, the gem the list socketed most in that colour (`sockets` in the generated item table are WoWSims' colour codes, 1 = meta). Before this the drop inherited whatever sat on the replaced piece — three empty sockets on a T6 chest read as a sidegrade. The piece the raider already wears keeps its own fitting (so "the item you already have" still sims to a zero delta), and a spec without a WoWSims list falls back to inheriting. `fitting` on the sim result says which.

**Gems and enchants do reach the simulation** — measured, not assumed: on a T6 shadow-priest reference set they are worth +108 and +125 DPS, so a run that silently dropped them would be obvious. Two things the binary gets wrong, both handled and both verified against the pinned release:

- ⚠️ **A gem it does not know aborts the entire run**, not just that item ("…had gem with id: N\nThis gem is not in the database"). Raiders socket things no caster table anticipated, so `engine.simulate()` strips the offending gem and retries, warning that the number is now slightly low. Without that, one exotic gem costs a raider their result completely.
- ⚠️ **It does not check a meta gem's colour requirement.** An inactive Chaotic Skyfire Diamond still contributed (1809 vs 1806 DPS in a run where it should have been dormant), so a raider who socketed wrongly would be simulated as if they had not. `loadout.js`'s `stripInactiveMeta()` removes it first, using the *same* rule as the gear check (`metaGemActive`) so the sim and a CLA report's "Meta-Gem inaktiv" finding can never disagree.

**Every raider's loadout can be exported** as a WoWSims "From JSON" import (`GET /api/lootcouncil/export?character=…`, `engine.buildIndividualExport()`), so anyone can paste it into wowsims.github.io/tbc and check the number themselves. It is built from the same pieces as the headless run and reproduces its DPS exactly (verified: 1813/2757/2149 both ways) — an export that quietly differed would be worse than none, because it would make the page look wrong. ⚠️ The WoWSims individual import does **not** switch class from the JSON, so the payload carries `simUrl`: the sim page it belongs on. Pasted on another class's sim it produces nonsense without complaining.

## Rollen und Specs (#669)

The council's roles are **caster, healer, tank, melee and ranged** (`ROLES` in `config/councilSpecs.js`). They are not the setup's roles (`gameVersions/classes.js`: tank/healer/melee/ranged): the council splits ranged damage by what it scales with, so a shadow priest is a `caster` (spell power) and only the hunter is `ranged` (agility, attack power) — they never want the same item.

| Role | Specs (WCL key) | BiS list | Simulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| caster | Priest-Shadow, Mage-Arcane/Fire/Frost, Warlock-Destruction/Affliction/Demonology, Druid-Balance, Shaman-Elemental | WoWSims (Fire/Frost borrow Arcane, Affliction/Demonology borrow Destruction) | yes |
| healer | Priest-Holy/Discipline, Druid-Restoration, Shaman-Restoration, Paladin-Holy | Wowhead (Discipline borrows Holy) | no |
| tank | Warrior-Protection, Paladin-Protection, Druid-Guardian (bear) | WoWSims | no |
| melee | Warrior-Fury/Arms, Rogue-Combat/Assassination/Subtlety, Druid-Feral (cat), Shaman-Enhancement, Paladin-Retribution | WoWSims (Assassination/Subtlety borrow Combat) | no |
| ranged | Hunter-BeastMastery/Marksmanship/Survival | WoWSims (Marksmanship borrows Beast Mastery) | no |

- **Every class/spec of the rule set has an entry** (`test/config/councilSpecs.test.js` holds it against `gameVersions/classes.js`), so a raider is only off the council when their *spec* is unknown. A class whose specs all share one role is placed by class alone (`CLASS_FALLBACK`: mage, warlock, rogue, hunter); a warrior, paladin, priest, druid or shaman without a spec is not. `specFor()` ignores spaces in the spec, so "Beast Mastery" finds `Hunter-BeastMastery`.
- **Planned role** (`council-roles.json`, the role switch in the raider dialog) knows all five roles; `specForRole()` takes the first spec of the class in that role, which is the raid build (a warrior planned as melee is Fury, a druid planned as tank the bear). The route accepts any id of `ROLES`.
- **Stored category views** (`council-views.json`): `VIEW_ROLES` is `[...ROLE_IDS, ""]`. A view stored before #669 only ever holds caster, healer or "" and stays valid; an unknown role still falls back to the default (`caster`).
- **Stat weights** are starting values, documented with their reasoning at the tables in `councilSpecs.js`: physical damage on the attack-power scale (strength 2.2 for strength classes = 2 AP × Kings, agility ~2 for rogues/cats/hunters, hit 1.8-2.2 below the cap, expertise ~2, crit/haste 1.4-1.8, armor penetration 0.2-0.35), tanks on the stamina scale (defense 1.5 first, dodge 1.2, parry 1.1, block rating/value only for shield tanks, armor small per point except for the bear, whose item armor counts five times). A spec can carry its own table (`weights`), otherwise its role's (`ROLE_WEIGHTS`). They order the candidates while nothing is simulated; the page shows no estimated gain from them.
- **Physical hit cap**: 9 % against a level-73 boss = 142 rating (15.77 per %), less talented hit — Fury 3 % (Precision), every rogue 5 % (Precision), Enhancement 6 % (Dual Wield Specialization), Survival 3 % (Surefooted), protection paladin 3 % (Precision). Arms, Retribution, Beast Mastery, Marksmanship, cat and bear are assumed without hit talents.
- **Which set is theirs**: `gearProfile.js` reads a set as `tank` (defense + dodge + parry + block rating ≥ 40; every WoWSims tank list sits at 63-561, every other list at 0), `physical` (attack power, strength, agility, physical hit outweigh spell power at least twice) or, as before, `caster`/`healer`. Tank comes first, because a protection paladin's T6 set carries 668 spell power and 125 spell hit and would otherwise read as a caster's. `fitsRole()` compares the council role's gear family (`gearFamilyFor()`: melee and ranged → physical), so a fury warrior's newest log in his tank set is passed over for his DPS set, and a balance druid's feral night no longer counts as caster gear. The roster's mismatch badge names the set (`gear.setRole` → Heilgear / Caster-Gear / Tank-Gear / Nahkampf-Gear).
- **No simulation yet for tanks, melee and hunters.** wowsimcli can sim physical DPS, but the bot vendors only caster rotations (`APL_FILES` in `scripts/fetch-wowsims-data.js`) and caster buff sets (`utils/wowsims/presets.js`). `isSimSupported()` asks for a non-healer spec with a `talents` string and a vendored APL, so switching one on is: fetch its APL, transcribe its WoWSims preset 1:1, add `talents`/`specField` to the spec, verify against the pinned binary. Until then they are suggested by need and labelled "nicht simuliert", like the healers.
- **The addon formats v1/v2 carry casters and healers only** (`LEGACY_ROLES` in `councilSync.js`; docs/loot-import.md). Version 3 (#670) adds the new roles.
