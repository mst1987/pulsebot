## Fight timelines and recommendations (the report page's "Kampfverlauf" and "Empfehlungen" tabs)

The log-check report (`/r/<id>`) carries a **time axis** since Sept 2026. `utils/logcheck/fightTimeline.js` builds one row per boss fight (bounds, `encounterId`, deaths with the killing blow); the analyzers that follow write into it — `raidDebuffs.js` (debuffs on the boss, stacks, expectation **from the roster**, never from the encounter), `totems.js` (per shaman: drops, the party buff the log actually saw, Windfury twisting; ⚠️ **the Anniversary client logs no totem aura at all** — no Windfury, Grace of Air, Strength of Earth … on anybody, only the proc "Windfury Attack" 25584 and Totemic Mastery (verified on `dWmfHY9CLQnjxKNk`, Sept 2026) — so a buffed totem whose buff never shows up is judged from its drops (`derived` on the row, the twisting and `wfDerived` on the summary, which the Totems table names in the tooltip): standing until the next drop in its slot, its duration or the shaman's death, Windfury via `windfuryBands()` with a pulse every 5 s and a 10-s buff that runs on after another air totem replaced it. Before that fallback both enhancement shamans read 0 % Windfury and one "gap" per fight; now 90–92 %), `cooldownTimeline.js` (presses vs. possible, Bloodlust windows), `mechanics.js` (avoidable hits, deaths judged avoidable/early/near-end/repeat), `activityTimeline.js` (GCD-covered bands, holes with a reason), `healers.js` (per healer: overheal per spell, the mana curve with every potion/Innervate/Mana Tide marked at the level it came, dispels with reaction time, and the shields/HoTs on the *active tank* — WCL's listed tank who took the most damage), `raidBuffs.js` (per player: which class buffs were on them — `full`, `late` (set after the pull, then kept), `partial` (not throughout: ran out or had a hole) or `none` — with the expectation from *that fight's* roster: no paladin means no blessing is missing, one paladin means one blessing per player, and Might on a priest is a wrong one, not a present one, while Light and Sanctuary are `neverWrong` in `config/raidBuffs.js` because TBC raids put them on everyone; a roster player whose buffs table was not fetched is not judged rather than reported as lacking everything; the bands come from WCL's buffs table with `sourceid`, which for auras is the unit the buff is **on**. ⚠️ **The Anniversary client does not put Fortitude and Mark of the Wild into the combatant info**, so WCL shows no band for them at any pull — only a re-cast mid-log makes one — while the blessings, Arcane Brilliance, Prayer of Spirit and Shadow Protection are listed (verified against a real Hyjal/BT log: 0 of 25 in the combatant info, 3 of 25 with a stray band). `untrackedBuffs()` therefore picks out a class buff that sits on fewer than half the raid at the *typical* pull (the lower median over the boss fights — "at any pull" was the first rule and one wipe broke it: a death removes every aura, WCL draws a band from the pull to the death for each, so the wipe fight showed Fortitude on all 25 and every other fight on nobody, which read as 24 raiders without Fortitude on fifteen bosses). Such a buff is **read off the raw buff events instead** (`inferBands()`, one filtered `getAllEvents` walk: the buff's own ranks plus every other raid buff's `removebuff` as death witnesses): a `removebuff`/`refreshbuff` proves it was on the player up to that moment, since the last point it was known to be off; an `applybuff` proves it from then on; and a death that stripped other buffs but not this one (52 of 58 deaths in that log strip; the rest were not seen) proves it missing and anchors the next remove. What the events do **not** see is the re-buff after a wipe — WCL keeps no events outside its fights, and a raid re-buffs out of combat — so after a remove the buff is `unknown` until the next event, never `none`: a player with no evidence in a fight gets the status `unknown`, shown as "?" / "nicht nachweisbar", tallied apart (`unknown` on every cell, row and player, `unknownCells` on the summary) and never a finding. The page says so above the matrix ("Aus dem Verlauf abgeleitet") and on the fight's Buffs topic; `inferred` on the fight and summary names the keys. Only when the events walk fails does the buff stay out of the judgement as before (`untracked`, "im Log nicht nachweisbar"). Verified on the same log over all 16 bosses: Fortitude 333 cells present, 0 missing, 66 open; a buff half the raid demonstrably had stays a finding when it is missing). Every one of them stores **derived intervals only**, never raw events, and judges a player until their death, not until the fight's end. Their raid summaries are own CLA fields (`CLA_FIELDS` in `report.js` — a field missing there is wiped by an RPB re-run). The "Raid-Debuffs" tab shows `raidDebuffs.rows` as the summary and, under it, a debuff × boss matrix that `report/panels/debuffs.js` derives purely from `timeline.fights[].debuffs` (mean over a boss's tries, tries in the tooltip; the expectation is the raid-wide `expected` flag each fight row carries, so a boss without a row for the debuff reads "–") — nothing is stored for it.

The page draws them with `src/web/report/charts.js`: pure inline-SVG builders (ribbon, marker, bar, line), no chart library, icon column and value column as HTML beside a horizontally scrolling plot on a **fixed 6 px/s scale**, every chart with a table twin.

**Three head levels, badges, tiles, one expand control** (Sept 2026, design signed off): a *card head* (`.vcard > summary`, a 2-px line under it when open) says who or what — boss, raider, raid section; a *part head* (`.part-head`, a tinted band with an icon `tile()` in the topic's tone, the title, the breadcrumb, one action) says which topic; a *table head* (`table.idx th`, tinted, mono, uppercase) says which column. Sentences like "2 Tries · Wipe bei 32 % · Kill 3:24 · 1 Tod" are `badge()`s with an icon and a tone (ok / mid / bad / accent; `count` for the round counter form), the section buttons carry their tone on the counter (`.sec .n.mid/.bad`) instead of a dot, and every `<details>` summary ends in `expBtn()` — "Details" plus a round 30-px chevron button that fills and turns when open — because the old ▸ glyph was too small to find. **Lists of things that belong to somebody are grouped under that somebody** (`groupedTable()`): the Cooldowns, Mechaniken and Totems topics of a fight show one `<details class="grp">` per player (class tile, name, a result badge such as "3 von 4 genutzt" or "6 Treffer"), sorted by what needs attention, opened only where something was missed — a flat "Dorn · Bloodlust, Brokk · Shield Wall, Dorn · Mana Tide" list was unreadable. **The Heilung topic is a ranked healer list** (`.hlist`, one `<details class="hrow">` per healer): rank, class icon and name, the one healing + overheal bar, mana-low and dispel badges, hints (absorb, death, missing potion); the chips, the spell table and the "Manaverlauf öffnen" button sit in the row's body, the strongest healer open. All bars keep their fixed column width (`.bar` 120 px, `.bar-heal` 260 px), so a short spell name never makes a longer bar.

**Tooltips are the page's own box, never the browser's.** `static/report.js` ships one floating `#tip` element driven by `data-tip` (the head) and `data-tip-sub` (the explanation) on any element, HTML or SVG — hover, keyboard focus and a tap on touch all open it. There is no `title=` attribute and no SVG `<title>` left on the report pages, on purpose: the native box appears after a second, cannot be styled and does not work on touch. Every headline number explains itself through it — the KPI cards (`kpi()`), the raider badges, the healer chips and the table heads — so a raid lead does not have to know the analyzers to read the page. A `"Label: detail"` tooltip is split into head and sub rather than crammed into one line. **Numbers a reader compares within a column are WCL-style bars** (`barCell()` / `barPct()`): the number sits on a bar whose length is its share of the column's maximum (the healing spell and healer tables) or the percentage itself (activity, cooldown usage), toned like the charts, so the ranking is visible without reading digits. `report/fight.js`'s `fightParts()` documents the shape each topic expects and returns, per topic, a compact **table** and the **chart** separately — which half goes where is the page layout's business, not the topic's.

**The report page is three views, not sixteen tabs** (`renderReportPage`, Sept 2026, design signed off by the raid lead): a head with the title, the zone/date kicker, the actions and four KPI cards (`kpiCards()`: bosses with kills/wipes, deaths with the avoidable ones from `report.mechanics.deaths`, flask/elixir coverage, raid buffs — mean coverage of the expected buffs and how many were lückenhaft), then **„Das Wichtigste“** (`renderKeyFindings()`, redesign Okt 2026): the raid's findings, high impact first, five in view and the rest behind "n weitere Punkte anzeigen", each a `recItem()` row (impact badge, title, up to two evidence badges, status badge, the three verdict icon buttons approve / reject / edit, the text and "Eigenen Text schreiben" / "Regeltext zeigen" in the foldable body; a second click on the active verdict takes it back) — and, for a reviewer, **the page's one main button "Empfehlungen senden …"**, a `detailDialog` (`dlg-rs-send`) around `renderSendBox`. A reader sees the approved findings and no section while there are none. The open-recommendations KPI is gone on purpose: "294 offene Empfehlungen" in the head overwhelmed more than it said. Then a segmented switch **Raid · Bosse · Raider** driven by the same `data-show` script as everything else (`static/report.js`), the open view kept in the url hash (`#raid` / `#bosse` / `#raider`; `#boss-<key>` opens that boss's page; `#raider-<name>` or `?player=<name>` opens that raider's card — `static/report.js`). Bosse opens first when there is a timeline, Raid otherwise. There is still **no JS bundle** on these pages: `<dialog>` elements plus one plain script, `/r-assets/report.js` (see "The report pages' files" below), the CSRF token fetched from `/api/session`.

**The raid DPS/HPS strip and the boss-health line come from the WCL v2 API, and only from there.** `utils/logcheck/fightSeries.js` writes `timeline.fights[].series = { step, dps, hps, bossHp }` (5-s buckets, compact arrays of equal length — a ten-minute fight is 121 values each; a council fight adds `bossHpTargets` per boss, the line itself is their mean), which `report/fight.js`'s `fightSeries()` draws above the topic switch. **The same `graph` answer carries one series per source, so `series.players = [{ name, type, dps?, hps? }]` keeps every roster player's own curve on the same buckets at no extra request** (mapped through `idToPlayer` by actor id, a pet added to its owner only where the series names one — WCL folds pets into their owner by default — and every other source dropped); the player page's `playerSeries()` draws it against the raid mean per player (raid series ÷ players with a curve) with the own death and Bloodlust as marks, and `summarizeFightSeries()` writes `report.fightSeries` (own CLA field): per raider the share of the fight (alive) their output sat below half their own mean, the "Einbrüche" chip and the `series.dips` recommendation (DPS only, thresholds in `recommendationRules.js`). The data is the binned `report.graph` (DamageDone/Healing, `classes/warcraftlogsV2.js` documents the schema pieces used) and, for the boss health, the *enemies'* damage events with `includeResources: true` — a boss swings every couple of seconds, so a few hundred events give a sample every few seconds where the friendly damage events would be tens of thousands. Three things are deliberate: the `graph` values are calibrated against the series' own `total` (`asRate()`), because the schema does not say whether a point is a rate or the amount per bin; boss health is *measured*, never interpolated — a bucket holds the last sample the log saw, the first bucket takes the first sample rather than an assumed 100 %, and only a kill's final bucket is forced to 0; a log without hit points (no advanced combat logging) leaves `bossHp` null instead of a line drawn from `fightPercentage`. **The access is optional and lives in the settings** (Einstellungen → Verbindungen → *Warcraft Logs*, `config.warcraftlogsV2`, the secret masked like the Anthropic key): without it the series is `null`, the tab says once what is missing, nothing fails. **The v1 key in `.env` stays for everything else** — the v2 client is not a replacement, it fetches exactly what v1 cannot bin.

**Healers are judged by their own yardstick** (`config/healerSpells.js` holds the aura and energize ids, `config/recommendationRules.js`'s `healers` block the thresholds). Three things there are deliberate: a healer's activity is only a finding far below the DPS threshold and their holes never are — waiting is the job; "dispellable" is defined by the log itself (an aura somebody dispelled once counts, an application of it that ran its course untouched is a missed dispel), because a per-boss table would be wrong more often than the log; and the mana curve reads both event shapes WCL has used (`classResources` lists and `sourceResources` objects) and says *no curve in the log* when neither is there, rather than drawing a flat line.

**Recommendations** (`utils/logcheck/recommendations.js`, thresholds in `config/recommendationRules.js`) are plain rules over the finished report — per raider and for the raid, with impact, text and evidence; a missing source yields nothing, never a false "alles gut". They are rebuilt on every build; what the raid lead approved or rewrote lives beside them in `recommendationReview` and survives the rebuild. **Nothing reaches a raider unapproved**: `web/logcheck/recommendationSend.js` DMs only approved points (character → Discord account from `raiderCharactersStore`, ambiguous names reported, an unchanged set never sent twice), and `utils/logcheck/recommendationText.js` only *phrases* them with Claude (key in Einstellungen → Verbindungen; the key never goes back to the browser) — the raid lead's own text beats the model's, the model's beats the rule's.

## The report pages' files (#423)

`/r/<id>` and `/r/<id>/p/<n>` are server-rendered and public, and they are the only place where the raid lead reviews, rephrases and sends the recommendations. Until Sept 2026 all of it sat in one `src/web/report/render.js` of 3873 lines, with some 70 kB of CSS and fourteen inline scripts pasted into every page. Now:

- **`src/web/report/render.js` is a facade** with the old exports (`renderReportPage`, `renderPlayerPage`, `renderNotFound`, `renderError`, `layout`, `esc`, `authBar`, `themeToggleBtn`) — `server.js`, `docsPage.js` and `eventPublicPage.js` require it unchanged.
- **`src/web/report/`** holds the pages, one module per part, and the require graph has no cycle: `layout.js` (the shell, `esc`, the login bar, the theme toggle), `widgets.js` (icons, badges, tiles, bars, metric cards, dialogs, number formats; the class colours come from `utils/setupView`), `panels/*.js` (the detail tables: `consumables`, `potions`, `debuffs` with the debuff × boss matrix, `buffs`, `healers`, `rpbRoles` / `damage` / `rpb` for the RPB, `summaries` with `AREA_HOW`), `fightTopics.js` (outcome, deaths, mechanics, the compact topic tables, `groupByBoss`) and `fight.js` (`fightParts()`, `fightSeries()`, `playerSeries()`, the chart dialogs, `fightStats`), `bossView.js`, `raidGroups.js` (one function per area card, `AREA_GROUPS` gives their order), `raiderView.js` (raider card, paperdoll, send dialog), `recommendations.js`, `context.js` (`reportContext()`), `reportPage.js` and `playerPage.js`. Times on the pages ("gesendet …", "KI-Formulierung vom …") go through `utils/time/index.js`'s `formatGermanDateTime()`, i.e. in `TIMEZONE`, not the machine's zone.

## The game version of a log (#746)

Every log and every report knows its game version, decided once by `services/logcheck/logVersion.js`; what the analysis does with it is the version's rule set (next section, #747).

- **Which version:** (1) the raid the log is linked to (`versionOfEvent`), (2) else the **host of its link**: the version whose Warcraft-Logs site (Einstellungen → Spielversion, the origin of `wclUrlTemplate`, `versionLinks(v).wclSite`) is that host. Which host belongs to which version is a setting, never a table in the code; a host no version names (`classic.`, `www.`) or one two versions share tells nothing. (3) Else TBC (`LEGACY_VERSION`). A log or report stored before #746 has no version and counts as TBC (`recordVersion`).
- **Stored on the log** (`logStore`): `host`, `hostVersionId` (what the host told at detection, `""` = nothing; read once and kept), `versionId` and `versionFrom` (`event` | `host` | `default`). `linkEvent(..., { versionId })` gives the log its raid's version while linked, `unlinkEvent` goes back to the host's. `matchableEvents.eventLinkFields` hands the event's `versionId` on; every matchable event row carries one (`versionOfEvent`).
- **Stored on the report** (`report.js`): `versionId`, `reportHost`, and `reportUrl` on the host the link was posted with, else on the site of the version (`logVersion.reportLink`), else none. There is no fixed `classic.warcraftlogs.com/reports/` link anywhere any more (report, report list, `reportStore.metaOf`, the forced re-run).
- **The v1 client per version:** `new WarcraftLogs(key, { site })`; the log check builds it with `wclClientFor(versionId)`. The log rule set may fix its v1 site (`apiSite` in `utils/logcheck/rules.js`; every other version asks the site of its settings, `wclApiSite`): TBC keeps the **classic** host the CLA port always asked (it serves the fresh reports too), so nothing changes for TBC. `parseReportLink()` (`utils/logcheck/logLinks.js`, also `WarcraftLogs.parseReportLink`) returns `{ host, id }`; `parseReportId()` stays the id-only shortcut.
- **The clear refusal:** `buildReport` resolves the version first and throws `UnsupportedVersionError` (`unsupported: true`, `versionId`) for a version without rules — before any request to Warcraft Logs, so a Forever log never ends in "HTTP 404, stimmt der Link?" or an empty page. The web shows its German message („Logs von WoW Forever werden noch nicht ausgewertet.“); the bot (`/logcheck`, the log-channel buttons, the forced run) words it through `tr()` in the reader's language (`logevalForce.unsupportedCard`, catalog `config/botText/de/logcheck.js`). The title backfill and the message under a log post ask Warcraft Logs only for a version with a site.
- **Forced re-run:** the *Trotzdem auswerten* button of `/logcheck` carries the link's host as a fifth customId part (`logcheck-force:id:<id>:all:<host>`); without one the run takes the link of a log tracked under that id, else the bare id (TBC).
- **Report page:** `renderReportPage`/`renderPlayerPage` render inside `widgets.withReportVersion(report, …)`, so Wowhead, spell and armory links follow `report.versionId` (an old report: TBC). The version colour is #742.
- **Log → raid:** `logEventMatch.candidatesFor` keeps only raids of the log's version when its host told one (`knownLogVersion`; an event without a version stays in). For a log of unknown version the version of its channel's category (`categoryVersion`) ranks the raids after the category match and decides an otherwise ambiguous pair (`sameVersion`). `autoLinkLogs` and the automatch route hand the logs in with their channel's category for that. Linking by hand to a raid of another version is refused (`logVersion.linkConflict`: route `log-link`, `manualLog`).
- **Raid groups (#811):** the raids of a group start together in the same category, so time and category never tell them apart — such a log is ambiguous and the matching offers every raid of the group (candidates carry `raidGroupIndex`). One sign decides it: the log's title names the raid's number ("Zul'Aman 2", "ZA #2", "Raid 2 …" → Raid 2; `logEventMatch.siblingPick`/`raidNumberOf`). A log linked to a further raid counts for the group's one night in attendance. Test: `test/services/logcheck/logEventMatch.raidGroup.test.js`.
- **When it is linked:** right at detection (`handleLogMessage` → `autoLinkLogs`), then by the 10-minute sweep (`logAutoLink.js`) and once more right after the Discord login (`bot.js` ClientReady — the job's own first sweep runs at web start, before the login, when there are no guilds yet). `matchableEvents.loadMatchableEvents` offers the raids up to **2 h ahead** (`LOG_WINDOW_BEFORE_MS`, `getPastEvents(from, { untilSeconds })`, `ownMatchableEvents(..., { untilSeconds })`), the same window the match allows before a start: a log posted at the pull, minutes before the start time, is linked at once. Until 11.10.2026 only raids that had already started were offered, so such a log waited for the start plus the next sweep.
- **The list per version (#741):** `GET /api/cla?version=` lists one version (`logVersion.inVersionList`, handed to `prepareClaList` as `inVersion`): a log without a raid under the versions it can be from (`logVersion.listedVersions`: the link's host, else the version its raids exist in — `raidContent.versionsOfContent`, read from `raids` or the title; SSC/TK/Gruul are TBC only, Hyjal TBC and Forever —, a pinned channel category deciding between those, under every version only when nothing tells; „nicht zugeordnet"), a linked log only under its raid's stored version, a report without a log under its own; the counts follow. Candidates, `autoMatchCount` and `POST /api/cla/log-automatch?version=` take only raids of that version and only the logs its list shows; `linkConflict` refuses a raid of a version the log's raids do not exist in. The history's log list (`/api/history`) uses the same rule.
- Tests: `test/services/logcheck/logVersion.test.js`, `test/classes/warcraftlogs.test.js` (`parseReportLink`, site), `test/utils/logcheck/report.test.js` ("the version of a log"), `test/stores/logStore.test.js`, `test/services/logcheck/logEventMatch.test.js`, the `/logcheck` command tests and `test/web/report/widgets.test.js`.

## Logs per server (#920)

A log belongs to exactly one Discord server: `guildId` on the log (#917 gives it, with `guildFrom` and the origin server `postGuildId`). Every list of logs shows **only the active server's** (`l.guildId === activeGuildFor(req)`); a log without a server shows on none — the old fallback `!l.guildId ||` (such a log on every server) is gone.

- **Where the filter sits:** `GET /api/cla` (Log-Auswertung list and counts, automatch), `/api/history` (log list), the dashboard (`dashboardData.loadRecentEvents` / `annotateUpcomingExtras`), the past raids (`raidListing.loadPastRaids`), the raid detail's unlinked logs (`raidDetailView`) and the auto-link (`logAutoLink`, per guild). **Without an active server** every one of these answers an empty list (`/api/cla`, its automatch, `/api/history`) — never every server's logs.
- **Which server a log gets:** a log from a log channel gets the channel's server (as before); a log pasted on a raid (`manualLog.linkLogByUrl`) gets the **raid's server** (`guildOfEvent`), also as origin, so it stays there after an unlink — the active server is only the fallback for a raid whose server is unknown. Linking sets the raid's server (`eventLinkFields`), unlinking goes back to `postGuildId`.
- **Linked servers** do not see the logs of their partner's raids. The report pages `/r/<id>` are unchanged (a link opens on any server).

### The report rule (`services/logcheck/reportGuild.js`)

An evaluation (report) belongs to one server too. **One rule, used by every report list:** when a log points at the report (`log.reportRefId`), the **log's** `guildId` decides — a log that moves to another server takes its evaluation along; otherwise the report's own `guildId`. A report with neither is on no server. `reportGuildResolver(logs)` returns that lookup, `reportsOfGuild(reports, guildId, logs)` the reports of one server (`[]` for `""`). Both read only the metadata of `reportStore.listReports()` (`id`, `guildId`), never a full report.

- **Where it is stored:** `buildReport(link, { guildId })` writes `report.guildId` (`finishReport`); a merged page (second half of the same log) keeps the server it already had. Without a `guildId` the field stays away, so the golden master is unchanged. Callers: the web form (`POST /api/cla`, active server), `/logcheck` and the forced build (`interaction.guildId`), the log channel (`log.guildId`).
- **Migration:** at start, `settingsMigration.migrateReportGuilds` (right after `migrateLogGuilds`) gives every report that has **neither a log nor a `guildId`** the first Discord server (`firstServerId`), via `reportStore.assignGuilds` (idempotent, touches only reports without a server). Reports with a log need nothing — the log decides.
- **Where it is used:** the link reports in `GET /api/cla` (`prepareClaList` with `opts.guildId`), the dashboard tile "latest evaluation" (`dashboardData.loadLatestReport({ guildId, versionId })`), the slash command `/report` (`interaction.guildId`; outside a server nothing), and the loot council's log gear (`logGearStore.recentLogs` / `loadLogGear` with `guildId`: only the active server's logs, none without one).
- **Deliberately not filtered:** lists that follow a person or a character across servers — `characterEvidence`, `characterInfo`, `rosterAttendance`, `charGear`, `charGearIssues`, `profileLogs`, the organizer and `web/loot/lootCouncil.js`. #926's later steps decide those.
- **Known gap (#925):** the write routes of `/api/cla` that take a `logId` (link, unlink, evaluate, delete) do not yet check that the log belongs to the active server; an admin who knows another server's log id can still act on it. #925 closes this.
- Tests: `test/web/apiRoutes/cla.test.js`, `history.test.js`, `test/web/dashboard/dashboardData.test.js`, `test/services/events/raidListing.test.js`, `test/web/events/raidDetailView.test.js`, `test/services/logcheck/logAutoLink.test.js`, `test/web/logcheck/manualLog.test.js`; the report rule: `test/services/logcheck/reportGuild.test.js`, `reportList.test.js`, `test/stores/settingsMigration.reportGuilds.test.js`, `test/commands/lookup/report.test.js`, `test/stores/logGearStore.test.js`.

## Rule sets per version (#747)

`utils/logcheck/rules.js` is to the log check what `rulesFor()` (config/gameVersions) is to the signup: `logcheckRules(versionId)` returns a version's rule set, `null` for a version without one (Classic, an unknown id — a stored record's version goes through `recordVersion()` first, so one without a version is TBC).

- **What a rule set holds:** `classes` (WCL class names) and `classDefs` (the gameVersions classes with their specs), `roleForClass(class, counts)` (the RPB role), `analyzers` (which of `CLA_ANALYZER_KEYS` and `rpb` run, in run order — `CLA_ANALYZER_KEYS` and `report.js`'s `CLA_ANALYZERS` are held together by `rules.test.js`), `data` (the tables: `consumables`, `gear` (gems, sockets, enchants), `raidBuffs`, `raidDebuffs`, `totems`, `healerSpells`, `rpb`, `potions`, `drums`, `shadowResistance`, `zoneTags`, `excludedEncounterIds`, `hasteRatingPerPercent`, `bloodlust`), `ready`, `missing` (what still lacks) and `leftOut` (analyzer → why it does not run).
- **TBC is today's modules, unchanged.** Every table is the very object the analyzers already read (asserted by identity in `rules.test.js`); the tables an analyzer used to keep inside itself (`POTION_TYPES`, `DRUM_TYPE`, `LUST_IDS`/`LUST_MS`, `SR_ENCHANT`/`SR_GEM`, `ZONE_TAGS`) moved, unchanged, to `config/logcheckTables.js` (the analyzers read them there and still export them), so `rules.js` requires config only and never an analyzer (`common.js` and `rpb/common.js` require it; `test/web/noCycles.test.js` would catch a cycle). The analyzers still read their own modules directly; a version with its own data hands its tables in through `ctx.rules.data` (each `CLA_ANALYZERS` entry gets `ctx`) when it comes.
- **Golden master:** `test/utils/logcheck/report.golden.test.js` builds a whole TBC report from every real analyzer over a deterministic fake client (`test/helpers/fakeWcl.js`) and compares it byte for byte with `test/fixtures/reportBuildGolden/tbc-report.json`, captured before the rule sets. A deliberate content change: `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 npx jest test/utils/logcheck/report.golden.test.js`.
- **Where the rule set is read:** `buildReport` runs only `rules.analyzers` (an analyzer that does not run leaves its field `null`, the report keeps its shape), checks gear only with `data.gear`, and hands the rule set to `selectPlayers(table, rules)` (a class outside the nine is kept when the rule set knows it), `buildGearIssues`, `analyzeShadowResi`, `resolveSituationalGear` and `analyzeRpb` (`roleForClass(class, counts, rules)`: the nine classes keep the RPB's switch (`classicRole`), a class only the rule set knows is placed by its specs (`specRole`: tank/healer by majority, else the damage spec WCL named, by its `buffRole`)). `analyzeRaidProgress` and `analyzeFightTimeline` get the report's `versionId` (raidContent's version-scoped lookups — a TBC log naming Ragnaros is no Molten Core raid any more; the title backfill does the same with the log's version). `VALID_CLASSES` stays TBC's class list for the callers outside the log check.
- **Report page:** `reportContext()` carries `ran` (`analyzersRan(report)`: the analyzers of the report's version). Sicht Raid leaves out an area whose analyzer did not run (`AREA_ANALYZER` in `raidGroups.js`), the boss table drops the "v2-Zugang" note when the fight series does not run, the raider card says nothing about potions when they are not counted.
- **KI-Formulierung:** the system prompt names the game of the report's version (`systemPrompt(versionId)`, the rule set's label; `SYSTEM` is TBC's), no more fixed "TBC Classic".
- **Whether a version can be read** is `logcheckCapability(versionId) → { possible, reason, message, missing }` (in `services/events/versionCapabilities.js`, re-exported by `logVersion.js`): rule set ready **and** a Warcraft-Logs site known (`wclApiSite`). Reasons: `unknown_version`, `no_rules`, `rules_not_ready` (with `missing`), `no_site`. It is the "possible" of the feature `logcheck` of the per-version matrix (#761, docs/game-versions.md "Funktionen je Version"); `logcheckSupported()` asks that feature, so a log check switched off in Einstellungen → Spielversion refuses like an unsupported version, with its own text (`unsupportedMessage`: "Logcheck ist für … ausgeschaltet (Einstellungen → Spielversion).").

**WoW Forever** has a rule set with `ready: false`, Classic-derived where it is cheap and certainly right (facts: #736):

- the nine Classic classes, specs and roles (`gameVersions.rulesFor("forever").classes`), the same role mapping;
- the analyzers without the TBC-only ones (`FOREVER_LEFT_OUT`): **drums** (no drums before TBC), **totems** (Tranquil Air, Windfury and Grace of Air no longer stack — no twisting, which is what the analyzer measures), **shadowResi** (Mother Shahraz) and **rpb** (spell ranks, haste rating, Kalecgos, TBC trash requirements). Sunder stays (still five stacks), as do consumables, potions, boss uptimes, timeline, fight series, raid debuffs, cooldowns, mechanics, activity, healers and raid buffs;
- what Classic settles: `excludedEncounterIds: []`, `hasteRatingPerPercent: null`, `bloodlust: null`.

**Still missing before Forever can be enabled** (`FOREVER_MISSING`, the capability's `missing`): the Forever Warcraft-Logs site (unknown, Einstellungen → Spielversion), and the data — consumables with Classic ids, Classic potions and runes, enchant rules without gems, raid buffs with Forever's reach (many group buffs are raid-wide there), raid debuffs at Classic ranks, healer auras and mana sources, class cooldowns/trinkets/engineering with Classic ids, avoidable damage per Forever boss. Each analyzer in Forever's list then reads its table from `ctx.rules.data` instead of its TBC module, and `ready` becomes `true`.

## The unfinished-raid guard (CLA / RPB)

An evaluation of a raid that is still running is worth little and unfair to the raiders it judges — half the bosses have no kill to measure against and every further pull changes the numbers. So `buildReport()` refuses one, and every path into it inherits that: `/logcheck`, the log-channel buttons, the admin menu.

- **The rule** is `src/utils/logcheck/raidProgress.js`: which raids a report covers (boss names via `tbcContent.js`, plus the report's own zone) and whether each one's final boss (`FINAL_BOSSES` there) was *killed*. Karazhan ends at Prince Malchezaar — Nightbane is optional and regularly skipped. German and English encounter names both count, since WCL hands back whatever the uploading client called them.
- **It errs towards letting things through**: a raid it cannot identify (unknown zone, no final boss listed) is never blocked. Being unable to see the end of a raid is not evidence that it has not happened, and a guard nobody can get past is worse than one that misses a case.
- **Two exceptions** (Oct 2026): a log **without a single kill** is never finished, even in an unknown zone (a log posted at the first pull used to pass as "Raider: 0"); and a tracked log linked to an **own raid** also waits for that raid's planned instances (`plannedIds`, from `logChannel.plannedInstances`) — "Hyjal + BT" with BT not entered yet is not over. Raid-Helper events name no instances and add nothing.
- **The refusal is cheap**: `buildReport` checks right after the fight list — the one request it has already made — and throws `IncompleteRaidError` before the dozens of calls the analysis would spend. Callers branch on `err.incomplete`, **not** `instanceof`: every one of them mocks `report.js` in its tests, and a mock without the class turns `instanceof` into a TypeError.
- **Getting past it is deliberate, never accidental.** `buildReport(link, { force: true })` is the way, and both front ends ask first: in Discord the refusal carries a *Trotzdem auswerten* button whose click opens a **modal** that wants `JA` typed in (`commands/logcheck/logevalForce.js` — the button and the modal share one customId prefix and one handler, branching on `isModalSubmit()`; a modal cannot be shown after `deferReply`, which is why the click cannot evaluate directly). In the admin menu the job comes back `incomplete` together with `raids` (`raidProgress.raidSummary()`: per raid the encounter list with what lies), and `lib/raids/confirmIncomplete.ts` turns that into the "Raid noch nicht abgeschlossen" dialog with the boss grid (`components/raid/IncompleteRaid.tsx`) and repeats the call with `force`.
- A report built anyway keeps `raidProgress` on it, so the page says the raid was not finished instead of the reader having to remember.
- **The same summary drives the Log-Auswertung list** ("Hyjal 3/5", yellow while the final boss stands). It is one list (`reportList.prepareClaList`: every log plus the reports built from a link that no log points at, filters `all|open|unlinked|done`); for a log nobody evaluated yet the raids come from its WCL fight list, read once together with the title (`logTitles.backfillLogTitles`, re-exported by `logChannel`) and stored on the log (`logStore.setLogRaids`). A page view asks WCL for at most 3 logs, a failed report rests 30 min (in memory), and `logAutoLink`'s 10-minute sweep fills the rest (`backfillAllLogTitles`, newest first, at most 20 per run). An unfinished raid is read again only while the post is under 12 h old and at most every 10 min — a raid that was called off stays unfinished forever. Karazhan counts 11 encounters: the opera is one, the rare spawns none.

## Progress for the raid plan (#534)

`GET /api/raidplan/progress` (docs/raidplan/sharing.md, "The plan follows the raid") tells the raid plan which of its bosses are
down, which one is being fought and which comes next. Two modules:

- **`utils/logcheck/bossProgress.js`** (pure): `deriveProgress(fights, sections)` over a v1 `report/fights` answer and the plan's
  bosses in section order (`raidplanStore.bossesForInstances`, no "Allgemein", no trash). A fight counts only with `boss` > 0 and
  is matched to a section by `tbcContent.encounterKey()` of its name **or** of `config/bosses.js`' `bossName(boss)` — the offset
  Anniversary ids (50601, 100623) fold back there, so an untranslated German fight name still finds its boss. `encounterKey()` also
  folds the two WCL names that differ from the table: "Reliquary of Souls" → "Reliquary of the Lost", "Daakara" → "Zul'jin" (which
  fixes the same miss in `raidProgress.js`' encounter grid). **Killed** = a fight with `kill: true`; a wipe changes nothing.
  **Current** = the section of the report's *last* fight while it runs (`inProgress: true`, which v1 carries on a live log, or no
  `kill` yet); trash after it ends it. **Next** = the first boss not killed, in the plan's order, **starting in the instance of the
  most recent boss pull** (a raid doing BT before Hyjal while the plan lists Hyjal first is followed through BT), then over all
  sections; null when everything is down.
- **`services/raidplan/raidplanProgress.js`**: the report is the newest log linked to the event with a report id
  (`logStore.listLogsForEvent`, filled by `logAutoLink.js` or by hand). **WCL v1** `getFights` with the bot's key — v2 would need
  the optional OAuth client. Only inside the raid window (`eventStartMs` − 30 min to + 6 h): outside it no request at all. One
  request per report and 60 s (`CACHE_MS`), shared by every page that polls (one promise per report); a failure — private report,
  429, no key — is cached as "no data" for the same minute and answered as `live: false`.
